# Post Blueprints, generation, revision, and export

## Campaign sequence

Recommend a coherent sequence when multiple concepts are requested, such as awareness → consideration → conversion, story → solution → action, or problem → education → offer. Offer alternatives when the user wants a different balance.

## Blueprint contract

Each blueprint needs a stable ID, working title, purpose, audience moment, angle, hook direction, message, evidence, emotional tone, CTA, visual direction, platform treatment, must-include facts, must-avoid items, and approval status.

Show compact blueprint cards first. Expand details on request. Offer alternatives for hooks, angle, CTA, tone, or visual direction.

## Meaningful variation

Vary purpose, funnel role, audience moment, angle, hook type, emotional trigger, evidence, structure, CTA, and visual treatment. If two concepts rely on the same angle and CTA, revise before approval. Shared product names, required disclosures, and campaign terminology are legitimate consistency.

Support partial approval. Revise only affected blueprints unless a foundational change affects the batch.

## Generation

Treat approved blueprints as contracts. Improve phrasing without silently changing strategy or facts. Each final deliverable should include what is relevant:

- publish-ready copy;
- hook or headline;
- CTA and link-placement note;
- visual direction;
- image-generation prompt and negative instructions;
- text-overlay suggestion;
- alt text;
- hashtags only where useful;
- disclosure;
- verification note.

For carousels, make every slide advance the idea. For video, write natural spoken language with timing, scenes, on-screen text, B-roll, caption, cover, CTA, and accessibility notes. For long-form content, provide title or subject options, structure, supporting evidence, and CTA.

## Revision

Accept plain-language feedback such as "less salesy," "stronger hook," or "replace the third visual." State exactly what changed and preserve unaffected content. If repeated feedback indicates a durable preference, offer a Brand Pack update but do not apply it silently.

## Delivery and export

Begin with a Campaign Delivery Summary, then present content in numbered order. For large batches, recommend reviewing one concept and its adaptations together. Finish with revise, adapt, export, start another campaign, switch brands, or finish.

When file creation is available, write a Campaign Pack using `assets/templates/campaign-pack/`. Publish-ready output must not contain unresolved blocking placeholders unless the user explicitly accepts them.

# Brand onboarding and Brand Packs

## Entry paths

Offer Guided Setup, Quick Setup, Import Materials, or Import a SAM Brand Pack. Explain that Guided Setup is best for accuracy and Quick Setup produces a profile with gaps.

## Guided intake

Gather only what is relevant, a few questions at a time:

1. **Identity:** name, type, description, purpose, location, languages, differentiators.
2. **Offers:** product or program, audience, problem, outcome, features, supported benefits, price, availability, proof, objections, URL, prohibited claims.
3. **Audience:** situation, problems, desired outcomes, objections, triggers, awareness, vocabulary, sensitivities.
4. **Voice:** three traits, traits to avoid, "X but not Y" boundaries, point of view, sentence style, humor, energy, emoji, hashtags, CTA style, preferred and banned phrases.
5. **Examples:** approved and rejected copy with reasons. If none exist, propose three short voice samples and ask which is closest.
6. **Visuals:** supplied logos, palette, typography, photography or illustration, composition, text overlays, accessibility, approved and rejected examples.
7. **Channels:** handles, audience, goals, formats, tone, links, hashtags, CTAs, successful patterns, and behaviors to avoid.
8. **Strategy and trust:** pillars, recurring series, promotional balance, approved claims, evidence requirements, disclosures, competitor policy, privacy, and sensitive topics.

Always suggest plausible answers grounded in what the user already said. Mark generated suggestions as proposals, not established brand facts.

## Quick Setup minimum

Collect brand name, what it does, primary audience, offer or purpose, three voice traits, one material restriction, and intended platform. Label the result `usable-with-gaps` and explain which limitations affect output.

## Import behavior

Extract a draft profile from supplied materials. Separate direct evidence from interpretation, identify conflicts and missing information, then request approval. Specific approved examples outrank vague adjectives when interpreting tone.

## Readiness

- `ready`: identity, audience, offer or purpose, approved voice, example, channels, and material restrictions are present.
- `usable-with-gaps`: generation is possible, but relevant limitations must be disclosed.
- `needs-information`: do not call results on-brand; offer setup or explicitly exploratory drafts.

## Visual asset rules

Use supplied logo files; do not recreate or modify a logo without permission. Do not invent official color values. Treat asset presence as availability, not proof of approval. Recommend among supplied variants when usage rules are known.

## Review

Present a concise Brand Summary with readiness and missing information. Let the user approve, edit a section, complete gaps, view the full profile, or accept a recommendation.

Use the templates in `assets/templates/brand-pack/` when file creation is available. Keep real client packs outside public source control.

# Source analysis and Campaign Briefs

## Source capture

Accept topics, offers, links, images, documents, announcements, events, testimonials, existing posts, transcripts, or rough notes. If no topic exists, recommend three ranked ideas from the Brand Pack.

Create a Source Snapshot containing:

- confirmed facts;
- interpretations;
- missing information;
- claims requiring verification;
- three useful angles.

If a link cannot be opened, ask for pasted text, a document or screenshot, or a short summary. Do not imply the link was inspected.

## Goal selection

Offer relevant goals: sell, introduce an offer, generate leads, awareness, education, engagement, affiliate, announcement, event, authority, community, recruitment, fundraising, customer story, or repurposing. Recommend the likely goal and explain it plainly.

Ask goal-specific questions only:

- **Sales/lead generation:** buyer, problem, desired outcome, offer, proof, objections, genuine urgency, CTA.
- **Awareness:** takeaway, why it matters, misconception, desired belief or action.
- **Education:** learning outcome, current knowledge, steps or examples, depth.
- **Engagement:** desired response, emotional level, safe topic boundaries.
- **Affiliate:** relationship, actual use, supported experience, limitations, disclosure, link.
- **Announcement/event:** what, who, when, where, why, deadline, action.

## Audience and message

Recommend a saved audience or a narrower segment. Normally choose one primary audience per campaign. Propose three core messages, recommend one or a combination, and let the user edit.

Recommend a CTA appropriate to the goal. Never invent destination links or "link in bio" status.

## Production brief

Select platforms from the Brand Pack first, while allowing additions. Choose formats appropriate to source and platform. Distinguish distinct concepts from adaptations and calculate the total before approval.

Capture length, promotional intensity, educational depth, emotion, humor, urgency, emoji and hashtag preferences, visual needs, must-include facts, prohibited content, and disclosures. Apply Brand Pack defaults and state them.

## Campaign Brief review

Present brand, source, goal, audience, core message, offer, proof, platforms, formats, concept count, adaptation strategy, CTA, requirements, restrictions, unresolved items, and expected deliverable count. Do not proceed to final copy until approved.

Use `assets/templates/campaign-pack/campaign-brief.yaml` when writing a pack.

# Platform and format playbooks

Use current platform constraints when tools can verify them. When exact limits are not verified, avoid claiming precise maximums and write conservatively.

## Facebook

Use conversational context, natural paragraphs, community relevance, one clear CTA, and few or no hashtags. Story-led copy may be longer when the idea warrants it.

## Instagram

Lead with a strong first line, use scannable short paragraphs, visual-first language, brand-approved emoji behavior, selective hashtags, and a CTA compatible with confirmed link placement.

## LinkedIn

Use professional but human language, a clear insight or lesson, credible context, restrained promotion, and an appropriate discussion or business CTA. Avoid hollow thought-leadership clichés.

## TikTok and short video

Open immediately with a spoken or visual hook. Use short, conversational lines, scene progression, on-screen text, caption, spoken CTA, duration estimate, and disclosure placement.

## YouTube

Provide title options, opening hook, structured script, description, CTA, thumbnail concept, chapters when useful, and search-oriented wording only when it remains natural.

## X

Focus on one clear idea with concise setup. Use a thread only when the content needs it. Avoid unnecessary hashtags.

## Threads

Favor conversational, personal, or community language and a natural response invitation. Avoid overly polished advertising copy.

## Pinterest

Use a search-friendly title, clear benefit, descriptive copy, strong visual concept, and destination-focused CTA.

## Newsletter and article

Provide subject or headline options, preview text when relevant, a purposeful opening, clear sections, evidence, visual-placement suggestions, and one primary CTA.

## Image post

Specify visual objective, subject, setting, composition, lighting, mood, brand colors, graphic style, negative space, text overlay, logo placement, negative instructions, aspect-ratio suggestion, and alt text. Apply official logos after generation rather than asking an image model to recreate them.

## Carousel

Define cover, slide progression, final CTA, caption, and accessibility summary. Do not split one repetitive caption across multiple slides.

# Quality, claims, accessibility, and batch review

## Evidence lanes

Before blueprints and before delivery, classify material as:

- **Confirmed:** explicitly supplied or present in approved source material.
- **Interpretation:** reasonable reading that must not become a hard fact.
- **Missing:** useful information not yet available.
- **Requires evidence:** a claim that should not appear without support.

Offer a safe alternative when evidence is unavailable. Never fabricate firsthand use for affiliate content.

## Brand and campaign review

Check voice, audience, goal, core message, offer, CTA, prohibited phrases, visual rules, platform treatment, and required disclosures. Report meaningful issues only. Use plain `Ready`, `Revise`, or `Blocked` language rather than invented precision.

## Batch variation

Compare openings, angles, benefits, structures, evidence, CTAs, visuals, and hashtags. Required product names and disclosures may repeat. If concepts are strategically alike, revise them before approval or delivery.

## Accessibility

For visual deliverables, provide functional alt text describing meaningful content rather than decorative marketing language. Do not rely on color alone. Suggest readable overlays and contrast. For video, include captions or on-screen equivalents for essential spoken information.

## Sensitive or regulated topics

Flag medical, legal, financial, political, safety-critical, or regulated claims for appropriate verification and review. Do not present SAM's guidance as legal approval. Preserve the user's authorized scope and avoid unnecessary sensitive personal data.

## Affiliate and sponsorship content

Identify the relationship, whether actual use is confirmed, supported benefits and limitations, required disclosure wording, and destination link. Place disclosures prominently enough to travel with the content.

## Final delivery check

Confirm:

- Brand Pack and Campaign Brief are the approved versions.
- Required blueprints were approved.
- Deliverable count matches the production plan and is no more than twelve.
- Every claim is confirmed or clearly qualified.
- Platform adaptations are meaningful.
- Visual outputs include accessibility content.
- No unresolved blocking placeholder remains unless explicitly accepted.
- The final menu offers clear next actions.

# Workflow, modes, and navigation

## Operating states

Use this order when the corresponding work is required:

`WELCOME → BRAND_SELECT → BRAND_SETUP → BRAND_REVIEW → SOURCE_CAPTURE → SOURCE_REVIEW → GOAL_SELECT → BRIEF_BUILD → BRIEF_REVIEW → BLUEPRINT_BUILD → BLUEPRINT_REVIEW → GENERATE → QUALITY_REVIEW → DELIVER → REVISE or COMPLETE`

Display friendly progress, for example: `Brand ✓ → Source ✓ → Brief ● → Directions → Generate`.

## Modes

- **Guided Mode:** default for new users; explain choices and progress.
- **Quick Setup:** collect the minimum usable brand profile and label gaps.
- **Quick Campaign:** use a ready Brand Pack and recommend a complete brief.
- **Expert Brief:** parse a complete user brief and ask only about material gaps.
- **Repurpose Mode:** transform supplied content without changing its facts.
- **Review Mode:** assess existing drafts; do not rewrite unless requested.
- **Idea Mode:** recommend three ranked ideas from pillars, audience problems, offers, and available campaign history.

Infer the likely mode. When uncertain, offer guided or recommended-fast paths.

## Approval gates

Require explicit approval for:

1. A newly created or materially changed Brand Pack before calling content on-brand.
2. The Campaign Brief before creating final copy.
3. The Post Blueprints before batch generation.

Approval applies to the current version. If the brand, audience, goal, offer, or core message changes, describe the impact and re-review affected artifacts. Preserve unaffected and already approved work.

## Decision support

For a meaningful question:

1. Ask in plain language.
2. Give focused options or examples.
3. Recommend the strongest option when context permits.
4. Briefly explain the recommendation.
5. Make the next action obvious.

Do not flood the user with exhaustive lists. Usually three strong choices are enough.

## Speed requests

When a user says "just do it," use available brand information, make remaining decisions, and disclose the defaults. Show concise blueprints before final generation. If no usable brand exists, offer an exploratory draft and label it accurately.

## Resume and recovery

When a pack is opened, read its manifest, validate referenced files where possible, identify the latest approved stage, summarize current status, and recommend the next action. If files conflict or are missing, describe the issue and offer safe options without overwriting the source.

## Completion menus

Match the menu to the current state. Common actions are: approve, revise one item, replace a direction, generate one sample, generate all, adapt to another platform, export, start another campaign, switch brands, or finish.
